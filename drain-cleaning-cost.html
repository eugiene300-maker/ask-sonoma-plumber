How Much Does Drain Cleaning Cost?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Costs & Decisions · Sonoma County ]
Answer

In Sonoma County and the greater Bay Area in 2026, a straightforward drain snaking or auger job typically runs about $150 to $400. Hydro-jetting to blast out roots and grease runs roughly $500 to $1,200, and a video camera inspection runs about $250 to $500. Clearing a main line through an existing cleanout is on the lower end; if the plumber has to locate or dig to access the line, or work through a roof vent, costs climb. North Bay labor rates run above the national average, so local prices tend toward the higher end of these ranges.

Most likely causes

  1. Which fixture and how deep

    A single sink or tub clog near the trap is cheap and quick. A blockage deep in a branch line or out in the main lateral takes more time, bigger equipment, and costs more.

  2. Access to the line

    An accessible ground-level cleanout keeps the price down. If there is no cleanout, or the plumber must pull a toilet, go up on the roof, or dig, labor rises.

  3. Method needed

    A cable/auger is the least expensive. Hydro-jetting costs more but is what actually removes roots and hardened grease. A camera inspection adds cost but prevents guesswork.

  4. Severity and cause

    Roots and compacted grease take longer to clear than a soft clog. If the camera reveals a break or belly, you are then looking at repair costs, not just cleaning.

  5. Local labor and emergency timing

    North Bay rates run above the U.S. average, and after-hours, weekend, or emergency calls carry premiums.

How to check it yourself

  1. Before you call, note whether it is one fixture or the whole house; a single slow drain is often a cheap DIY or minor snake job.
  2. Locate your exterior cleanout, since a plumber who can work through an existing cleanout will usually charge less than one who must gain access another way.
  3. Try safe DIY first for a single fixture (plunger, hot water, hand snake) so you only pay for a pro when it is truly needed.
  4. Ask for a flat-rate quote for the specific job rather than an hourly rate, and ask whether a camera inspection is included or extra.
  5. Ask whether hydro-jetting or a simple cable is appropriate; do not pay for jetting on a simple soft clog, but expect it for roots or grease.
  6. Get the diagnosis in writing, especially if a camera finds a deeper problem, so you can compare repair bids.

When to call a licensed plumber

Drain cleaning is generally a pro service once DIY plunging and snaking fail, when multiple fixtures are affected, or when roots or grease are suspected. Call a pro immediately, and expect main-line pricing, if several drains are slow at once, the lowest drain backs up, or sewage appears. It is worth paying for a camera inspection any time a main line has been cleared, so you learn whether the cause was a one-time clog or a recurring problem like roots or a pipe belly that will keep coming back.

How to prevent it

  • Clear single-fixture clogs yourself with a plunger and hand snake to avoid a service-call minimum.
  • Install a cleanout if your home lacks one; it lowers the cost of every future service.
  • Keep grease and food out of drains and use strainers to reduce how often you need professional cleaning.
  • For older homes with roots, budget for periodic maintenance jetting rather than waiting for an emergency backup, which costs more.
  • Bundle a camera inspection with a cleaning so you are not paying two separate trip charges.

Frequently asked questions

Why is hydro-jetting so much more than snaking?

A cable auger punches a hole through a clog, but hydro-jetting uses a high-pressure water jet that scours the full pipe wall, removing roots, grease, and scale. It requires specialized equipment and more time, which is why it costs more, but it lasts far longer for root and grease problems.

Do I really need a camera inspection?

Not for a simple one-time sink clog. But any time your main line backs up, a camera is worth it. It tells you whether you had a fluke clog or an underlying issue like root intrusion or a sagging pipe, so you do not pay to clear the same line repeatedly.

Why are North Bay prices higher than what I see online?

Most national price averages do not reflect Sonoma and Marin labor rates, which run above the U.S. average. Expect local quotes toward the upper end of published ranges.

Is a cheap $99 drain special a good deal?

It can be, but read the fine print. These specials usually apply only to an accessible cleanout and a simple cable job; roots, no cleanout, or a main-line problem will cost more, so confirm what is and is not included.
