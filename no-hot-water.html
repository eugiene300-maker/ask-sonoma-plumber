Why Do I Have No Hot Water?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Water Heaters · Sonoma County ]
Answer

No hot water usually traces to a specific failed part. On a gas heater it is most often a pilot light or thermocouple that has gone out, or the gas valve; on an electric heater it is a tripped breaker, a failed heating element, or a bad thermostat. If you have some hot water that runs out fast, the likely culprits are sediment, an undersized tank, or a broken dip tube. Check the simple items first, and call a pro for gas valve, wiring, or element work.

Most likely causes

  1. Gas: pilot light or thermocouple out

    If the pilot won't stay lit, the thermocouple (the sensor that proves the flame) is often dirty or failing. Relighting per the label instructions is safe; a pilot that keeps dying points to the thermocouple or gas valve.

  2. Electric: tripped breaker

    An electric water heater draws heavy current, and a tripped breaker or blown high-limit reset is a common no-heat cause. Reset the breaker once; if it trips again, stop and call a pro.

  3. Electric: failed heating element or thermostat

    Most electric tanks have upper and lower elements and thermostats. A burned-out lower element gives lukewarm or quickly-depleted hot water; a failed upper element can mean no hot water at all.

  4. Gas control valve failure

    If the pilot lights but the burner won't fire, the gas valve or its sensor may have failed. This requires a licensed plumber or gas technician.

  5. Sediment, dip tube, or undersized tank

    Hard-water sediment insulates the burner or element and steals capacity, a cracked dip tube lets cold water mix with the hot, and a tank that is simply too small runs out during heavy use. All of these show up as 'not enough' hot water rather than none.

Safe checks before you call

  1. Confirm it is the whole house, not one fixture: if only one faucet is cold, the problem is that fixture's valve or cartridge, not the heater.
  2. For electric units, check the breaker panel and reset a tripped water-heater breaker once.
  3. For electric units, press the red high-limit reset button on the upper thermostat cover (turn off the breaker first).
  4. For gas units, look through the viewport to see if the pilot flame is lit.
  5. If the gas pilot is out, follow the printed relight instructions on the tank exactly, and stop if you smell gas.
  6. Check that the thermostat is set around 120 F and hasn't been bumped to a low setting.
  7. Note whether you get no hot water at all or a small amount that runs out fast; this tells a plumber a lot.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if the breaker trips more than once, if you smell gas at any point (leave and call from outside), if the pilot won't stay lit after relighting, or if the burner won't fire. Gas valves, wiring, and heating elements involve gas and 240-volt power and should be handled by a professional. If your unit is also over 8-12 years old, ask whether repair or replacement makes more sense.

How to prevent it

  • Flush the tank annually to keep sediment from smothering the burner or lower element, especially with Sonoma hard water.
  • Test the pilot and burner before winter so you aren't caught cold during a valley freeze.
  • Keep the area around a gas heater clear so the flame gets proper combustion air.
  • Right-size the tank (or go tankless) if a growing household keeps running out of hot water.
  • Have the anode rod and elements checked during annual maintenance to catch wear early.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my hot water suddenly stop?

A sudden total loss usually means a tripped breaker or failed element on electric units, or a pilot light or gas valve issue on gas units. Check the breaker or pilot first, then call a pro if those don't resolve it.

Why do I run out of hot water so fast?

Fast-depleting hot water points to sediment buildup, a broken dip tube letting cold water mix in, a failed lower heating element, or a tank that is too small for your household. A flush or element replacement often restores capacity.

Is it safe to relight my own gas water heater pilot?

Yes, if you follow the printed instructions on the tank and do not smell gas. If you smell gas, leave the area and call your gas utility or a plumber; do not try to relight it.

Why is my water only lukewarm?

Lukewarm water usually means one of two heating elements has failed on an electric unit, the thermostat is set low, or sediment is insulating the burner. Confirm the temperature setting, then have the element or thermostat checked.

Could hard water in Sonoma County be causing my hot water problems?

Yes. Local hard water leaves mineral sediment that insulates burners and elements and reduces usable capacity, which is why annual flushing matters more here than in soft-water regions.
