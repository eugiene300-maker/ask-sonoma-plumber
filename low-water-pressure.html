Why Is My Water Pressure Low?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Pipes, Pressure & Fixtures · Sonoma County ]
Answer

Low water pressure usually comes from one of two places: a restriction at a single fixture (a scaled-up aerator or clogged cartridge) or a whole-house problem (a failing pressure-reducing valve, a partly closed shutoff, corroded galvanized pipe, or a clogged softener or filter). The fastest way to narrow it down is to ask whether the weak flow affects one fixture or the entire home. In many older Sonoma County homes, decades of hard-water scale and corroding galvanized pipe are the underlying cause.

Most likely causes

  1. Scaled or clogged aerator/fixture

    Sonoma County hard water leaves mineral scale and grit that clog faucet aerators, showerheads, and cartridges. If only one fixture is weak, this is the most common and easiest cause to fix.

  2. Failing pressure-reducing valve (PRV)

    Many local homes have a PRV at the main to protect against high street pressure. When it fails or drifts, it can throttle pressure to the whole house. Symptoms affect every fixture at once.

  3. Partially closed shutoff valve

    A main or fixture shutoff that is not fully open restricts flow. This often happens after plumbing work or meter service and is a quick, free thing to check.

  4. Corroded galvanized steel pipes

    Older Sonoma homes often have galvanized supply lines. Over decades the interior corrodes and narrows, choking flow. Pressure may be fine at the meter but weak inside, and often worse at hot fixtures.

  5. Municipal supply or well/pressure-tank issue

    City supply can dip during peak demand or main work. Homes on well water depend on the pump and pressure tank; a waterlogged tank or failing pump switch causes low or fluctuating pressure.

  6. Clogged water softener or whole-house filter

    A resin-fouled softener or a filter cartridge overdue for replacement adds resistance and drops pressure to the entire house.

  7. Hidden leak

    A significant leak in a supply line, irrigation, or under the slab bleeds off pressure and volume before water reaches your fixtures.

How to check it yourself

  1. Decide if it is one fixture or the whole house. One fixture points to that fixture; every fixture points to the main, PRV, softener, or pipes.
  2. For a single weak fixture, unscrew the faucet aerator or remove the showerhead and look for chalky white scale or grit. Soak it in white vinegar for a few hours, rinse, and reinstall.
  3. Confirm the main shutoff and the fixture angle stops are fully open (turn counterclockwise until they stop).
  4. Buy an inexpensive hose-bib pressure gauge and thread it onto an outdoor spigot. Normal residential pressure is roughly 40 to 80 psi. Below 40 suggests a supply, PRV, or restriction problem.
  5. If you have a whole-house filter or softener, check whether it is overdue for a cartridge change or in bypass mode, then retest.
  6. Compare hot vs cold at one sink. If only hot is weak, look at the water heater and its connections; if both are weak, the problem is upstream.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if the whole house is affected, if a gauge reads below about 40 psi or above 80 psi, if you suspect a PRV failure or galvanized corrosion, or if you hear running water with everything off (possible hidden leak). Pressure spikes above 80 psi can damage fixtures and should be corrected. If you are on a well and pressure surges or the pump short-cycles, have the tank and pump switch checked promptly.

How to prevent it

  • Clean aerators and showerheads a few times a year, or soak them in vinegar, to stay ahead of hard-water scale.
  • Consider a water softener or scale-control system to protect fixtures and pipes from Sonoma County minerals.
  • Have your PRV and static pressure checked periodically; replace a PRV proactively every 7 to 12 years.
  • Service or change whole-house filter cartridges on schedule.
  • If your home still has galvanized supply lines, plan for a repipe before repeated pinhole leaks and flow loss force an emergency.

Frequently asked questions

Why is only my hot water pressure low?

That points to the water heater or the hot side specifically: sediment buildup, a clogged heater nipple, or scaled hot-side lines. Cold pressure being normal rules out the main and PRV.

What is normal household water pressure?

Most homes run well between 40 and 80 psi. Below 40 feels weak; above 80 stresses fixtures and appliances and usually calls for a pressure-reducing valve.

Can hard water really lower my pressure?

Yes. Over time, scale narrows aerators, cartridges, valves, and even pipe interiors. It is one of the most common pressure complaints in hard-water areas like Sonoma County.

The whole neighborhood has low pressure. Is it my problem?

Probably not your plumbing. Temporary drops can come from municipal main work, hydrant flushing, or peak demand. Sonoma Water and your local provider can confirm planned work.
