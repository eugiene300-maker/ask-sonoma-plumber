What Causes a Sewer Line Backup?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Drains & Sewer · Sonoma County ]
Answer

A sewer line backup happens when your home's main drain line, the single pipe that carries all waste to the city sewer or septic, gets blocked. In older Sonoma County neighborhoods the number-one cause is tree roots growing into cracks and joints of aging clay sewer laterals. Grease buildup, flushed wipes, a sagging or collapsed pipe, and occasionally a problem in the city main are the other main causes. The telltale sign is that multiple fixtures back up at once and the lowest drain in the house floods when you run water elsewhere.

Most likely causes

  1. Tree-root intrusion (very common here)

    Roots seek moisture and enter through joints and hairline cracks in older clay or Orangeburg laterals, then grow into a dense mat that snags paper and grease. This is the leading cause of backups in older Santa Rosa, Petaluma, and Sebastopol homes.

  2. Grease and fat buildup

    Years of cooking grease congeal on the pipe walls, narrowing the line until solids can no longer pass. This is common where kitchen waste has gone down the drain untreated for a long time.

  3. Flushed wipes and non-flushables

    So-called flushable wipes, paper towels, and feminine products do not disintegrate. They catch on roots or rough spots and quickly build into a full plug.

  4. Pipe bellies, offsets, and collapse

    Ground settling, especially in areas with expansive soils or seismic movement, can cause a section of pipe to sag (a belly) or shift at a joint. Waste and sludge pool in the low spot and eventually block flow.

  5. Deteriorated old pipe material

    Clay, cast iron, and Orangeburg (a tar-paper pipe used mid-century) crack, corrode, and delaminate with age, creating rough interiors and entry points for roots and infiltration.

  6. City main blockage or surcharge

    Occasionally the blockage is downstream in the public main. If the city main surcharges, sewage can push back toward your lowest fixtures. This is the utility's responsibility, but it is diagnosed the same way.

How to check it yourself

  1. Stop using water immediately. Do not flush, run the washer, or run the dishwasher, since every gallon has nowhere to go and will surface at your lowest drain.
  2. Watch for the classic multi-fixture pattern: flushing a toilet makes a tub or shower gurgle or bubble up, or the washing machine draining causes a floor drain to overflow.
  3. Check your lowest and closest-to-the-sewer drains first (downstairs shower, floor drain, or ground-floor toilet); backups always show up at the lowest point.
  4. Locate your exterior sewer cleanout, a capped pipe (often white or black, 3 to 4 inches) near the house or along the lateral path. If water is standing at the cleanout, the blockage is downstream of it.
  5. If you carefully remove the cleanout cap and water gushes out, the main line is blocked and under pressure; this confirms it is a main-line issue, not a single fixture. Stand clear, as it may be pressurized.
  6. Note whether neighbors have the same problem. If several homes back up at once, suspect the city main and call the utility.
  7. Do NOT pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up main; it will not clear roots or a collapse and creates a hazard for the plumber.

When to call a licensed plumber

A sewer backup is a call-a-pro situation, not a DIY fix. Call right away if multiple fixtures back up, if you hear gurgling across drains, if sewage appears at any drain, or if water stands at the cleanout. Sewage contact is a health hazard, so keep people and pets away from any surfaced wastewater. A pro will clear the line and run a camera to identify whether it is roots, grease, a belly, or a collapse, which determines whether you need a cleaning, a spot repair, or a lateral replacement. If several homes on the street are affected, contact Sonoma Water or your city utility, since the city main may be at fault.

How to prevent it

  • If you have mature trees and an older clay lateral, schedule an annual camera inspection to catch root intrusion early.
  • Use a root-control treatment (foaming products or a pro service) on older laterals to slow regrowth between cleanings.
  • Never flush wipes, paper towels, or feminine products, even if labeled flushable.
  • Keep grease and fats out of every drain to avoid buildup in the main.
  • Install or maintain a proper cleanout so future service is faster and cheaper.
  • Consider a backwater valve if your home has previously backed up from the city main; it prevents sewage from flowing back into the house.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if it's my line or the city's?

Homeowners are responsible for the lateral from the house to the city main connection. If only your home is affected, it is almost certainly your lateral. If multiple neighbors back up at the same time, the city main is the likely culprit and you should call the utility.

Why are tree roots such a big problem in Sonoma County?

Many older homes still have original clay or Orangeburg laterals with joints every few feet. Roots from the area's large trees seek out the moisture and nutrients at those joints and grow inside, making roots the most common backup cause in older Santa Rosa and Petaluma neighborhoods.

Can I just keep snaking it every year?

Cabling relieves the symptom, but if roots keep returning or a camera shows a belly or cracked pipe, repeated snaking only buys time. At some point a trenchless liner or replacement is more cost-effective than annual clearings.

Is it safe to stay in the house during a backup?

You can stay, but stop all water use and avoid contact with any surfaced sewage. Ventilate the area, keep kids and pets away, and disinfect any affected surfaces after the line is cleared.
