How to Prevent Clogged Drains

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Drains & Sewer · Sonoma County ]
Answer

Most clogs are preventable with a few simple habits: keep grease and food out of the drain, put a screen over every drain to catch hair and scraps, and flush your drains with hot water regularly. Flush only toilet paper down the toilet, use enzyme cleaners instead of caustic chemicals for maintenance, and if you own an older Sonoma County home with a clay lateral, add root control to your routine. These low-cost steps prevent the buildup that leads to slow drains and expensive main-line backups.

Most likely causes

  1. Grease and cooking fat

    Fats poured down the kitchen sink cool and solidify on pipe walls, then catch food and grow into a blockage. This is the top preventable cause of kitchen and main-line clogs.

  2. Hair and soap scum

    Hair binds with soap and oils in tubs and showers to form mats in the trap. A simple screen stops most of it before it enters the pipe.

  3. Food scraps and coffee grounds

    Even with a disposal, starchy, fibrous, and granular waste accumulates downstream. Scraping plates into the trash prevents it.

  4. Non-flushable items

    Wipes, paper towels, cotton, and floss do not break down and snag in the line, especially where roots are present.

  5. Hard-water scale

    Sonoma County's hard water deposits mineral scale that narrows pipes over time; periodic flushing helps limit buildup.

  6. Tree roots in older laterals

    In older homes, roots enter clay lateral joints and catch everything that passes. Root control slows regrowth between cleanings.

How to prevent clogs (routine)

  1. Never pour grease, oil, or fat down any drain. Let it cool, collect it in a can or jar, and throw it in the trash. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing.
  2. Put a mesh strainer or hair screen in every sink, tub, and shower, and empty it into the trash regularly.
  3. Scrape food scraps and coffee grounds into the compost or trash rather than the disposal; run the disposal with plenty of cold water when you do use it.
  4. Once a month, flush each drain with a pot of hot (not boiling) water to keep grease and soap moving; boiling water can harm plastic pipe.
  5. Flush only the '3 Ps' down the toilet: pee, poop, and toilet paper. Keep a trash can in the bathroom for wipes and hygiene products.
  6. Use an enzyme or bacterial drain maintainer monthly instead of chemical drain cleaners; enzymes safely digest organic buildup without harming pipes.
  7. For older homes with clay laterals and mature trees, use a root-control treatment on a schedule and get periodic camera inspections.

When to call a licensed plumber

Prevention is mostly DIY, but bring in a pro for periodic maintenance if you own an older home with a clay lateral and mature trees, since a scheduled jetting and root control is far cheaper than an emergency backup. Call a pro right away, rather than continuing prevention steps, if you already have a slow main line, multiple slow fixtures, gurgling, or any sewage backup, because those are active blockages that need clearing and a camera diagnosis, not just maintenance.

How to prevent it

  • Keep grease out of every drain, always.
  • Screen every drain and empty screens often.
  • Do a monthly hot-water flush on each drain.
  • Use enzyme maintainers, never caustic chemical cleaners.
  • Flush only toilet paper; trash everything else.
  • For older laterals, schedule root control and periodic camera checks.

Frequently asked questions

Do enzyme cleaners actually work?

Yes, for maintenance. Enzyme and bacterial products slowly digest the organic grease, soap, and food film that leads to clogs, and they are safe for pipes and septic. They will not blast through an existing full blockage, so use them to prevent clogs, not to clear one.

Is it okay to run hot water to melt grease down the drain?

No. Hot water may soften grease briefly, but it re-hardens further down the pipe where it is harder to reach. Keep grease out entirely; use the monthly hot-water flush only for general maintenance on drains that stay grease-free.

My home is from the 1950s with big trees. What extra step matters most?

Root control plus a periodic camera inspection. Older clay laterals in Sonoma County are prone to root intrusion, and catching roots early with treatment and inspection prevents the expensive backups that come from letting them grow.

Are flushable wipes really a problem if the box says flushable?

Yes. Independent testing shows so-called flushable wipes do not disintegrate like toilet paper. They snag on roots and rough pipe and are a leading cause of clogs and backups, so keep them out of the toilet.
