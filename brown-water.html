Why Is My Water Brown or Rusty?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Water Quality · Sonoma County ]
Answer

Brown, yellow, or rusty water is almost always caused by iron and sediment getting stirred into your supply, and it is usually a nuisance rather than a health emergency. The key clues are whether it comes from hot water only, cold only, or both, and whether it affects one fixture or the whole house. Hot-only points to your water heater; all fixtures suddenly discolored often points to a disturbed municipal main or hydrant flushing; and slowly worsening discoloration in an older home can mean corroding galvanized pipes.

Most likely causes

  1. Water heater sediment or a rusting tank (hot side)

    If only the hot water is brown, sediment and rust have built up inside the water heater, common with Sonoma County hard water. A steel tank that is rusting internally is nearing the end of its life.

  2. Corroded galvanized steel pipes

    Older local homes often have galvanized supply lines that rust from the inside. This releases iron that tints the water, frequently worse on the first draw of the morning and at specific fixtures.

  3. Disturbed municipal main or hydrant flushing

    When the water utility flushes hydrants or repairs a main, iron sediment in the pipes gets stirred up. Water turns brown at all fixtures, usually clears within a few hours, and often affects neighbors too.

  4. Well water with iron or manganese

    Homes on wells can have naturally high iron (reddish-brown) or manganese (brown-black) that discolors water and stains fixtures and laundry. This is a source-water issue, not a plumbing failure.

  5. Nearby construction or a water main break

    Pressure changes from breaks or heavy use can pull sediment loose, briefly discoloring the whole neighborhood's water.

How to check it yourself

  1. Run the cold water only at a couple of fixtures for two to three minutes. If it clears, the cause was likely temporary sediment from the main or flushing.
  2. Compare hot vs cold. If only hot is discolored, focus on the water heater. If both are discolored, the source is upstream of the heater.
  3. Check one fixture vs the whole house. One fixture points to that line or fixture; every fixture points to the main supply or shared piping.
  4. Ask a neighbor or check with Sonoma Water or your local provider about hydrant flushing or main work in the area.
  5. If it is hot water, carefully drain a few gallons from the water heater's drain valve into a bucket and look for rust and grit (follow the manufacturer's safe draining instructions).
  6. Note whether the color is reddish (iron) or brown-black (manganese), which helps identify well-water mineral issues.

When to call a licensed plumber

Occasional brief discoloration that clears after running the tap is generally not an emergency. Call a licensed plumber if only your hot water is rusty (water heater), if discoloration keeps returning or worsens in an older home (galvanized corrosion), or if you also notice reduced pressure or metallic taste. Contact your water provider first if the whole neighborhood is affected. If water is persistently discolored, has a strong odor, or you are unsure it is safe, avoid drinking it and get the water tested. Well owners with recurring iron or manganese should have the source tested and treated.

How to prevent it

  • Flush your water heater once a year to remove sediment, especially with Sonoma County hard water.
  • Consider replacing an aging water heater before the tank rusts through and fails.
  • If your home has galvanized pipes, plan a repipe before corrosion causes chronic rusty water and leaks.
  • Install a sediment or iron filter if you are on well water with elevated minerals.
  • After any known main work or hydrant flushing, run cold water for a few minutes before using it for cooking or laundry.

Frequently asked questions

Is brown tap water safe to drink?

Short-term rusty water from iron and sediment is usually an aesthetic problem, not a health hazard, but it is best not to drink it until it clears. If discoloration is persistent, has an odor, or you are unsure of the cause, stop drinking it and have it tested.

Why is only my hot water brown?

That isolates the problem to your water heater. Sediment settles and the tank can rust internally, tinting only the hot side. A flush may help; a tank that is rusting through needs replacement.

My water was brown for an hour then cleared. What happened?

That pattern strongly suggests the utility flushed hydrants or worked on a main nearby, stirring up iron sediment. It typically clears on its own within a few hours.

Will it stain my laundry and fixtures?

Iron and manganese can stain white laundry and leave reddish or dark marks on sinks and tubs. Avoid washing whites during discoloration and rinse fixtures promptly.
