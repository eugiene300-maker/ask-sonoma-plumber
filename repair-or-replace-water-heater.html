Should You Repair or Replace Your Water Heater?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Costs & Decisions · Sonoma County ]
Answer

Use this simple rule: replace the water heater if it's older than 8-12 years, the tank itself is leaking, you've had repeated repairs, or a repair would cost more than half the price of a new unit; otherwise repair it if the problem is a single minor part and the unit is still young. A leaking tank is never worth repairing. When a heater is near the end of its expected life, putting money into it rarely pays off, and a new efficient unit usually makes more sense.

Most likely causes

  1. Age of the unit

    Tank water heaters typically last 8-12 years, and Sonoma County hard water often pushes failure toward the earlier end. If yours is in or past that window, lean toward replacement.

  2. Tank leak vs. part leak

    A leak from a valve or fitting is repairable, but water seeping from the tank body means the steel has corroded through and the unit must be replaced.

  3. Repair cost vs. replacement

    If a single repair would run more than roughly 50% of a new installed unit, replacement is the better value, particularly on an older heater.

  4. Repair history

    Repeated failures (elements, thermostats, valves) signal a unit near the end of its life. Replacing it ends the cycle of service calls.

  5. Performance and efficiency

    If you're constantly running out of hot water or your utility bills have crept up, a right-sized or higher-efficiency unit can pay you back over time.

How to decide repair vs. replace

  1. Find the unit's age: read the manufacture date on the rating label, or decode it from the serial number.
  2. Identify the failure: is it a repairable part (valve, element, thermostat, thermocouple) or the tank itself?
  3. If the tank is leaking from its body, plan to replace; a corroded tank cannot be safely repaired.
  4. Get the repair quote and compare it to an installed replacement price; if repair exceeds ~50%, replace.
  5. Count recent repairs: two or more in a couple of years favors replacement.
  6. Factor in age: at 8-12+ years, put repair money toward a new efficient unit instead.
  7. Consider whether your capacity or efficiency needs have changed before buying the same thing again.

How to prevent it

  • Flush the tank yearly (or twice yearly with Sonoma hard water) to extend its usable life.
  • Check the anode rod every 3-5 years so the tank corrodes less and lasts longer.
  • Keep the thermostat at 120 F to reduce scale and stress on the tank.
  • Note the install date somewhere visible so you can plan replacement before a failure floods your home.
  • Add water softening or scale treatment in hard-water areas to slow the corrosion that shortens tank life.

Frequently asked questions

When should I replace instead of repair my water heater?

Replace if the unit is over 8-12 years old, the tank is leaking, you've had repeated repairs, or a repair would cost more than half of a new installed unit. Repair if it's a single minor part and the heater is still relatively young.

How long should a water heater last?

A conventional tank heater lasts about 8-12 years, and a well-maintained tankless can last 20 years. Hard water in Sonoma County can shorten tank life, so annual flushing matters.

Is it worth repairing a 10-year-old water heater?

Usually not, unless the repair is small and inexpensive. At 10 years the tank is near the end of its life, so a major repair is often money better spent toward a replacement.

Does hard water shorten a water heater's life in Sonoma County?

Yes. Local hard water leaves mineral sediment that corrodes the tank and reduces efficiency, which is why local heaters often fail toward the earlier end of the 8-12 year range without regular flushing.

What is the 50% rule for water heaters?

If a repair costs more than about half the price of a new installed unit, replacement is generally the smarter choice, especially on an older heater. It avoids paying repeatedly for a unit that is likely to fail again soon.
