Signs You Have a Hidden Slab Leak

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Pipes, Pressure & Fixtures · Sonoma County ]
Answer

A slab leak is a leak in a water line running under your home's concrete foundation. Tell-tale signs include an unexplained jump in your water bill, a warm or damp spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, low pressure, and unexplained cracks or mildew. You can screen for it with a simple water-meter test: shut off all water and see if the meter still moves. Because a hidden slab leak can undermine your foundation, it is worth confirming and fixing promptly.

Most likely causes

  1. Unexplained high water bill

    A sudden, unexplained increase in usage with no change in habits is one of the most reliable early signs that water is escaping somewhere you cannot see, including under the slab.

  2. Warm spot on the floor

    A leak in a hot-water line under the slab can heat a section of floor. A patch that is noticeably warm underfoot, especially on tile, is a strong hot-line clue.

  3. Sound of running water with everything off

    If you shut off every fixture and appliance and still hear water running, hissing, or trickling, water may be moving through a hidden break in the lines.

  4. Low water pressure

    A slab leak bleeds off pressure and volume before water reaches your fixtures, so unexplained whole-house pressure loss can accompany a leak.

  5. Cracks in flooring or walls

    Water eroding or shifting soil under the foundation can cause new cracks in the slab, tile, drywall, or foundation itself.

  6. Moisture, mildew, or musty odor

    Damp carpet, warped flooring, mildew smell, or unexplained moisture at floor level can signal water escaping under the slab.

How to confirm it yourself (meter test)

  1. Turn off every water fixture and appliance in the house, including ice makers and irrigation.
  2. Find your water meter (usually near the street or sidewalk) and read it, or watch the low-flow leak indicator dial or triangle.
  3. Wait 30 minutes to an hour without using any water, then read the meter again. If it moved, water is escaping somewhere in your system.
  4. To narrow it down, close the main shutoff at the house and repeat. If the meter stops moving with the house isolated, the leak is on the house side, possibly under the slab.
  5. Walk the floors barefoot to feel for warm or damp spots, and listen with the house quiet for running-water sounds near floor level.
  6. Check for the softer signs together: a spiking bill, new cracks, mildew smell, or low pressure. Several at once raises the likelihood of a slab leak.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber promptly if your meter test shows water moving with everything off, if you have a warm or damp spot on the floor, or if you hear running water when the house is quiet. Treat it as urgent if you see active water pooling, rapidly spreading moisture, new foundation or floor cracks, or a water bill that has spiked sharply, since an undetected slab leak can erode soil and damage the foundation. Do not break into the slab yourself; professional leak detection avoids unnecessary demolition.

How to prevent it

  • Keep house pressure in the 40 to 80 psi range with a working pressure-reducing valve, since chronic high pressure stresses buried lines.
  • Address hard-water scale and, in older homes, plan to replace aging galvanized pipe before it corrodes through.
  • Read your water meter periodically so you notice unusual usage early.
  • Watch for the early signs, warm floor spots, musty smells, creeping bills, and act before minor leaks become foundation problems.
  • Consider a whole-home leak monitor or automatic shutoff device for early detection.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if it is a slab leak versus another leak?

The meter test tells you water is escaping; a warm floor spot points to a hot line under the slab specifically. A plumber uses acoustic and pressure equipment to confirm the exact location before any concrete is opened.

Can a slab leak damage my foundation?

Yes, over time. Water under the slab can erode or shift soil, leading to cracks and settling. That is why prompt detection and repair matter, even though the leak itself is hidden.

Is a slab leak covered by homeowners insurance?

Policies vary. Many cover resulting water damage and the cost to access the leak, but not the failed pipe itself. Check your specific policy and document everything.

How urgent is it?

A confirmed slab leak should be handled quickly. It wastes water, drives up bills, and risks foundation and mold damage the longer it runs. Active pooling or spreading moisture is an emergency.
