Hard Water in Sonoma County: Effects & Fixes

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Water Quality · Sonoma County ]
Answer

Hard water is water carrying dissolved minerals, mainly calcium and magnesium, and much of Sonoma County has moderately hard to hard water. The minerals themselves are not a health hazard, but they leave scale on fixtures, shorten the life of water heaters and appliances, spot your glassware, and can leave skin and hair feeling dry. The common fixes are a whole-home water softener, a salt-free conditioner, or targeted point-of-use filters, each suited to a different goal.

Most likely causes

  1. What hard water actually is

    As water moves through soil and rock it picks up calcium and magnesium. Hardness is measured in grains per gallon (gpg) or milligrams per liter. Water above about 7 gpg is generally considered hard, and many Sonoma County households fall in that range or higher.

  2. Scale buildup on fixtures and pipes

    When hard water is heated or evaporates, minerals precipitate out as scale, the chalky white crust on faucets, showerheads, and inside pipes. Over time scale narrows pipe interiors and clogs aerators.

  3. Shortened water heater and appliance life

    Scale settles at the bottom of water heaters and coats heating elements, forcing them to work harder and fail sooner. Dishwashers, washing machines, and coffee makers also wear faster on hard water.

  4. Spotting and film

    Hard water leaves spots on glasses and dishes and a film on shower doors and tile because minerals stay behind as water dries.

  5. Dry skin and dull hair

    Hard water reacts with soap to form a residue that can leave skin feeling dry and hair looking flat, and it makes soap harder to rinse fully.

How to fix hard water

  1. Test your water hardness first, either with a home kit or by requesting local water quality data, so you know what you are treating.
  2. For true hardness removal, install a whole-home ion-exchange water softener, which swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium.
  3. If you want less scale without adding sodium, consider a salt-free conditioner, which changes how minerals crystallize so they cling less, though it does not remove them.
  4. Add point-of-use filters (under-sink or pitcher) where you want better taste or contaminant reduction at the tap.
  5. Flush your water heater periodically to clear sediment and scale and protect its lifespan.

How to prevent it

  • Wipe down glass shower doors and fixtures to keep scale from setting.
  • Flush your water heater once a year to remove settled scale.
  • Clean faucet aerators and showerheads by soaking them in vinegar to dissolve mineral crust.
  • Use rinse aid in the dishwasher to cut spotting.
  • If you install a softener, keep the brine tank filled and let it regenerate on schedule so it keeps removing minerals.

Frequently asked questions

How does a water softener regenerate?

An ion-exchange softener uses resin beads that swap hardness minerals for sodium. Periodically the system flushes the beads with a strong salt (brine) solution to recharge them and sends the mineral-laden wastewater down a drain. This regeneration cycle is why softeners need a salt supply and a drain connection.

Is softened water safe to drink?

Softened water is generally safe for most people, though it adds a small amount of sodium. Households watching sodium closely can use potassium chloride instead of salt, or keep one unsoftened tap for drinking water.

What is the difference between a softener and a conditioner?

A softener removes calcium and magnesium through ion exchange, so water is genuinely soft. A conditioner leaves the minerals in but alters them so they form less sticky scale. Softeners fully solve hardness; conditioners reduce scale without salt or a drain.

Does hard water damage my well water plumbing differently?

Some Sonoma County wells also carry iron or sulfur along with hardness, which can stain fixtures and add odor. In those cases you may need iron filtration or a specialized system in addition to softening.
