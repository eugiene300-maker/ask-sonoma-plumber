How to Flush & Maintain Your Water Heater

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Water Heaters · Sonoma County ]
Answer

To flush a tank water heater, turn off the power or gas, let the water cool, shut the cold-water supply, connect a hose to the drain valve, and drain the tank into a safe location, then briefly reopen the cold supply to stir up and rinse out sediment before refilling. In Sonoma County's hard water, flush once a year at minimum and ideally every six months, since minerals build up faster here. While you're at it, check the anode rod every few years, test the T&P valve, and set the thermostat to 120 F.

How to flush your water heater

  1. Turn off the heater: set a gas unit to 'pilot' (or off) and switch off the breaker for an electric unit so the elements never run dry.
  2. Shut the cold-water supply valve on top of the heater and let the water cool for a couple of hours to avoid scalding.
  3. Connect a garden hose to the drain valve at the bottom and run it to a driveway, floor drain, or bucket at a lower level.
  4. Open a hot-water faucet somewhere in the house to break the vacuum and help the tank drain.
  5. Open the drain valve and let the tank empty; expect cloudy, sediment-filled water, especially with hard water.
  6. With the drain still open, briefly turn the cold supply on and off a few times to stir and flush remaining sediment until the water runs clear.
  7. Close the drain valve, remove the hose, reopen the cold supply, let the tank fill completely (air will sputter from the open faucet, then run steady), and only then restore gas or power.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if the drain valve won't close or leaks after flushing, if no water drains because sediment has clogged the valve, if the anode rod is seized and won't unthread, or if the T&P valve weeps after testing. If flushing produces very heavy rust or the tank is over 8-12 years old, ask whether it is time to plan a replacement instead. When in doubt, an annual professional maintenance visit keeps the job safe and the warranty intact.

How to prevent it

  • Flush every 6-12 months in Sonoma County because hard water accelerates sediment buildup.
  • Check the anode rod every 3-5 years and replace it once it's heavily corroded, so it protects the tank instead of the steel.
  • Test the T&P valve once a year by lifting its lever briefly to confirm it releases water and reseats.
  • Keep the thermostat at 120 F to limit scale, save energy, and reduce scald risk.
  • Consider a whole-home water softener or scale treatment to slow sediment and extend tank life in hard-water areas.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I flush my water heater in Sonoma County?

Because of the area's hard water, flush at least once a year and ideally every six months. Homes on well water or with visible sediment may benefit from more frequent flushing.

What temperature should I set my water heater to?

The Department of Energy recommends 120 F, which balances scald safety, energy savings, and reduced mineral scale. Higher settings raise scald risk and speed up sediment buildup.

How do I test the T&P relief valve?

Place a bucket under the discharge tube, lift the valve's lever for a second, and let it snap back. Water should flow and then stop cleanly; if it keeps dripping or nothing comes out, have it replaced.

What is an anode rod and why does it matter?

The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes in place of your steel tank. Checking and replacing it every few years is the single best way to extend tank life, especially in hard water.

Can flushing a very old water heater cause a leak?

Occasionally, yes. On a neglected older tank, sediment can be partly sealing small weak spots, and a flush may reveal a drip or a drain valve that won't reseal. If your unit is over a decade old and never flushed, consider having a pro do the first one.
