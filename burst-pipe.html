Burst Pipe: What to Do Right Now

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Emergency & Safety · Sonoma County ]
Answer

Shut off your main water supply immediately to stop the flooding, then open faucets to drain the remaining water from the pipes. If water is near outlets, the electrical panel, or appliances, shut off power to the affected area at the breaker. Contain and mop up water, take photos and video for your insurance claim, and call a licensed plumber right away. Turning the water off first is the single most important step to limit the damage.

Most likely causes

  1. Freezing temperatures

    When water inside a pipe freezes it expands and can split the pipe, often bursting when it thaws. In Sonoma and Marin this most often hits uninsulated pipes in crawlspaces, attics, exterior walls, and outdoor spigots during a cold snap.

  2. Corrosion and age

    Older galvanized steel and aging copper pipes corrode from the inside over decades, thinning the walls until they fail. A sudden burst is often the end of a slow, hidden decline.

  3. High water pressure

    Water pressure above about 80 psi stresses pipes, joints, and fittings. A failed or missing pressure regulator can push pressure high enough to rupture a weak point.

  4. Water hammer and stress on joints

    Repeated pressure surges when valves or appliances shut off quickly can loosen or crack fittings and soldered joints over time.

What to do right now

  1. Shut off the main water supply. Find the main house shutoff (often near the water heater, in the garage, or where the line enters near the front hose bib) and turn it fully clockwise, or use the curb meter shutoff with a meter key.
  2. If water is near electrical outlets, the panel, appliances, or light fixtures, do not stand in the water. Shut off power to the affected area at the breaker panel first, and if you cannot reach the panel safely, stay out and call for help.
  3. Open faucets, both hot and cold, and flush toilets to drain water out of the pipes and relieve pressure at the burst.
  4. If the burst is near the water heater, shut off its gas (turn the control to OFF) or electric breaker so it does not run dry.
  5. Contain the water: place buckets, towels, and a mop under the leak, move furniture and valuables out of the way, and lift rugs off wet floors.
  6. Document everything for insurance before major cleanup: take photos and video of the burst pipe, the standing water, and all damaged belongings and building materials.
  7. Start drying out the area with towels, a wet/dry vac, and fans to reduce the risk of mold once the water is off.
  8. Call a licensed plumber to repair or replace the pipe, and contact your insurance company to open a claim.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber as soon as the water is off, and call one immediately if you cannot stop the flow, if the burst is inside a wall or under a slab, or if it involves the water heater or main line. Call 911 or your utility if water has reached the electrical panel and you cannot safely cut power, or if you also smell gas. Standing water plus electricity is a shock hazard, so stay out of it until power is confirmed off.

How to prevent it

  • Insulate pipes in crawlspaces, attics, garages, and exterior walls before winter, and cover outdoor spigots with faucet covers.
  • During a hard freeze, let a faucet drip and open cabinet doors so warm air reaches pipes under sinks.
  • Have a plumber check your water pressure and install or replace a pressure regulator if pressure exceeds about 80 psi.
  • Replace aging galvanized or failing pipe before it bursts, especially in older Sonoma and Marin homes.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and keep a meter key handy so you can react in seconds.

Frequently asked questions

What is the very first thing to do with a burst pipe?

Shut off the main water supply. Stopping the flow immediately limits flooding and damage far more than anything else you can do.

Should I turn off electricity too?

Yes, if water is near outlets, the panel, appliances, or fixtures. Cut power to the affected area at the breaker, and never stand in water to reach a panel. If you cannot do it safely, stay clear and call for help.

Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Sudden and accidental burst pipes and the resulting water damage are commonly covered, while gradual leaks and lack of maintenance often are not. Document everything with photos and video and contact your insurer promptly.

Can I just tape or clamp the pipe myself?

A pipe clamp or repair tape can be a very temporary stopgap after the water is off, but a burst pipe needs a proper repair by a licensed plumber to avoid another failure and hidden water damage.
