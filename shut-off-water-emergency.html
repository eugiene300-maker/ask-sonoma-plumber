How to Shut Off Your Water in an Emergency

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Emergency & Safety · Sonoma County ]
Answer

You have three places to shut off water, from closest to the leak outward: the fixture shutoff (a small valve under a sink or behind a toilet), the main house shutoff (where the water line enters the home, often near the water heater, front hose bib, or in the garage), and the street/meter shutoff at the curb box. For most leaks, close the nearest fixture valve first. For a burst pipe or a leak with no obvious fixture, go straight to the main house shutoff. Turn valves clockwise (righty-tighty) to close, and if the leak involves a water heater, also shut off its gas or power.

What to do right now

  1. Identify how big the problem is. A dripping faucet or running toilet only needs the fixture shutoff. A burst pipe, flooding, or water with no clear source needs the main house shutoff.
  2. For a fixture: look under the sink or behind the toilet for a small oval or round handle (or a lever) on the supply line. Turn the round handle clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever a quarter turn so it is crosswise to the pipe.
  3. For the whole house: find your main shutoff. In Sonoma and Marin homes it is commonly near the water heater, in the garage, in a utility closet, or just inside where the main line enters, sometimes near the front hose bib. It is usually a round wheel (gate valve) or a lever (ball valve).
  4. Close the main: turn a round wheel valve fully clockwise, or rotate a lever valve a quarter turn until it is perpendicular to the pipe. Wheel valves may take several turns.
  5. If you cannot find or turn the main, use the street/meter shutoff. Find the meter box near the curb or sidewalk, lift the lid, and locate the valve on the street side of the meter. Most need a water meter key or a large wrench, turned a quarter to a half turn until the valve is crosswise to the pipe.
  6. Open the lowest faucet in the house (and an outdoor spigot) after shutting off to drain remaining pressure and water from the pipes and reduce leaking.
  7. If the leak is at or near the water heater, also shut off its energy: for a gas heater turn the gas control knob to OFF, and for an electric heater switch off its breaker at the panel. This prevents the heater from running dry and being damaged.
  8. Leave the water off and call a licensed plumber to locate and repair the problem before turning the supply back on.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if you cannot find your main shutoff, if a valve will not turn or will not fully stop the water, or if water has reached electrical outlets, the panel, or the furnace. If water is spraying near electrical wiring or you smell gas, leave and call 911. Do not force a badly corroded valve, since snapping it can make the flooding worse.

How to prevent it

  • Before an emergency, walk your home and physically locate all three shutoffs so you are not searching during a flood.
  • Tag the main shutoff with a label and keep a water meter key hanging nearby.
  • Exercise your main and fixture valves once or twice a year by closing and reopening them so they do not seize.
  • Consider upgrading old multi-turn gate valves to quarter-turn ball valves, which are faster and more reliable.
  • Know that the meter shutoff belongs to the water district, so use it only in an emergency and report if you damage it.

Frequently asked questions

Which way do I turn the valve to shut water off?

Round wheel valves close clockwise (righty-tighty), often taking several full turns. Lever (ball) valves close with a quarter turn until the handle sits crosswise, perpendicular to the pipe.

I can't find my main shutoff. Where should I look?

Check near the water heater, in the garage, in a utility or laundry closet, and along the wall closest to the street, often near the front hose bib. If you still cannot find it, use the meter shutoff at the curb with a meter key.

Do I need to shut off the water heater too?

If the leak involves the water heater or you shut off the whole house for a while, yes. Turn a gas heater's control to OFF or flip an electric heater's breaker so it does not heat an empty or draining tank and burn out.

The main valve is stuck. What now?

Do not force it hard enough to snap it. Go to the meter shutoff at the curb instead, then call a plumber to replace the stuck main valve so it works next time.
