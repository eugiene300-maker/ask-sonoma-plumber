Why Are My Pipes Banging? (Water Hammer)

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Pipes, Pressure & Fixtures · Sonoma County ]
Answer

A loud bang, thud, or hammering when you shut off a faucet or when an appliance closes a valve is usually water hammer: a fast-moving column of water slamming to a stop and sending a shock wave through your pipes. It commonly shows up when high water pressure, missing or dead water-hammer arrestors, and loose pipes combine. The banging is not just annoying; the repeated shock can loosen joints and fittings over time, so it is worth diagnosing.

Most likely causes

  1. Classic water hammer from fast-closing valves

    Solenoid valves in washing machines, dishwashers, and ice makers snap shut in milliseconds. The moving water has nowhere to go, so it hammers against the pipe and fittings. This is the most frequent trigger for a sudden bang.

  2. Missing or failed water-hammer arrestors

    Arrestors are small air-cushion shock absorbers installed near quick-closing valves. Older homes may never have had them, and cushion-style ones can lose their charge over the years, letting hammer return.

  3. High water pressure

    Pressure above roughly 80 psi makes hammer far louder and more damaging. Homes without a working pressure-reducing valve, or with a drifting PRV, are especially prone to it.

  4. Loose pipe straps and unsecured runs

    When pipes are not firmly strapped to framing, normal flow and pressure changes let them knock against studs, joists, and each other. This produces rattling and banging distinct from a single sharp hammer.

  5. Thermal expansion

    As a water heater heats water, the water expands. On a closed system with a check valve or PRV and no expansion tank, that pressure has nowhere to go and can cause creaking, ticking, or knocking noises.

  6. Trapped air in the lines

    Air pockets, common after supply work or a system that was drained, can cause sputtering and knocking until the lines are purged.

How to check it yourself

  1. Note when the banging happens. A bang right when an appliance stops filling points to water hammer; random rattling during flow points to loose pipes.
  2. Measure static pressure with a hose-bib gauge on an outdoor spigot. Above about 80 psi is too high and makes hammer worse.
  3. Try the traditional air-chamber recharge: shut off the main, open the highest and lowest faucets in the house to fully drain the lines, then close them and turn the main back on. This can restore air cushions that quiet older systems.
  4. Where you can see exposed pipes (basement, crawlspace, garage, under sinks), gently check that straps and clips are snug and add cushioned clamps where pipes are loose.
  5. Listen near the water heater for knocking that tracks with heating cycles, which suggests thermal expansion and a possible need for an expansion tank.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if the banging is loud and persistent, if your measured pressure is above 80 psi, if noise continues after you have recharged the air chambers, or if you hear knocking tied to the water heater (thermal expansion needs an expansion tank). Repeated water hammer can loosen joints and eventually cause leaks, so do not ignore a bang that keeps getting worse. Any work inside walls to add arrestors is best left to a pro.

How to prevent it

  • Keep house pressure in the 40 to 80 psi range with a properly working pressure-reducing valve.
  • Install water-hammer arrestors at washing machines, dishwashers, and ice makers, especially in newer construction with fast solenoid valves.
  • Add an expansion tank if you have a closed system with a PRV or check valve and a water heater.
  • Make sure pipe runs are strapped every few feet with cushioned clamps to prevent movement.
  • Have arrestors checked periodically, since cushion types can lose their charge over time.

Frequently asked questions

Is water hammer dangerous?

A single bang will not burst a pipe, but repeated shock waves can loosen joints and fittings and lead to leaks over months or years. It is a real maintenance issue worth correcting, not just a noise.

Why did the banging start suddenly?

Common triggers are a new appliance with a fast-closing valve, a PRV that has drifted high, air chambers that have waterlogged, or plumbing work that left the system without its usual cushion.

Will an expansion tank stop the noise?

It helps only if the noise is from thermal expansion on a closed system, typically knocking that tracks with the water heater. It will not fix classic appliance water hammer, which needs arrestors and correct pressure.

Can high Sonoma County water pressure cause this?

Yes. Higher street pressure and a failing PRV both amplify water hammer. Checking and correcting pressure is often the single most effective step.
