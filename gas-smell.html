Why Do I Smell Gas in My House? (What to Do Now)

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Emergency & Safety · Sonoma County ]
Answer

If you smell gas (a rotten-egg or sulfur odor added to natural gas), treat it as an emergency. Do not touch light switches, appliances, or anything electrical, do not use your phone inside, and do not light a match, candle, or flame. Get everyone, including pets, out of the house immediately and leave doors open as you go. From a safe distance outside, call 911 and PG&E's 24-hour gas emergency line at 1-800-743-5000, then have a licensed plumber inspect and repair the gas line before you go back in.

Most likely causes

  1. Loose or worn appliance connector

    The flexible connector or fittings behind a stove, dryer, water heater, or furnace can loosen over time or corrode, letting small amounts of gas escape. This is one of the most common sources of a persistent gas odor near an appliance.

  2. A pilot light that has gone out

    On older water heaters, wall furnaces, or ranges, a pilot light that blows out keeps the gas valve feeding fuel with nothing to burn it. The result is a gas smell around the appliance. Do not attempt to relight it until the area has been checked and cleared.

  3. Loose fitting or valve on the gas line

    Threaded joints, shutoff valves, and unions along the black-iron or CSST gas piping can develop small leaks, especially after remodeling, an earthquake, or vibration over years of use.

  4. Damaged or corroded gas piping

    Buried yard lines, pipes in crawlspaces, or lines hit during digging or renovation can crack or corrode. These leaks can be serious and are not something to investigate yourself.

  5. Non-gas odors that can be mistaken for gas

    Sewer gas (hydrogen sulfide from a dry trap or venting problem) and 'rotten-egg' smelling water from sulfur bacteria in a water heater can smell similar. Only assume it is one of these AFTER the gas company or fire department confirms there is no natural gas leak. When in doubt, treat it as a gas leak and get out.

What to do right now

  1. Do NOT flip any light switch on or off, unplug anything, or use any appliance, garage door opener, or thermostat. A single spark can ignite gas.
  2. Do NOT use your cell phone, landline, or any electronics while inside the house.
  3. Do NOT light a match, lighter, candle, or start a vehicle in an attached garage.
  4. Get everyone out immediately, including children and pets, and leave the doors open behind you as you exit to let gas escape.
  5. If you can do so safely and quickly on your way out, and you know where it is, you may turn the outside gas meter valve a quarter turn to off using a wrench. Never go looking for the source or into a crawlspace to find a leak.
  6. Walk to a safe location well away from the house, ideally a neighbor's home or across the street.
  7. From that safe distance, call 911 and PG&E's 24-hour emergency line at 1-800-743-5000.
  8. Do not go back inside for any reason until 911, the fire department, or PG&E tells you it is safe.
  9. After the utility clears the immediate danger, have a licensed plumber inspect and repair the gas line or appliance connection before gas service is restored.

When to call a licensed plumber

A gas leak is never a DIY repair. Call 911 and PG&E (1-800-743-5000) first from outside, and always use a licensed plumber for the repair and re-test. If anyone feels dizzy, nauseous, has a headache, or trouble breathing, or if you hear a hissing sound or see blowing dirt near a gas line, leave immediately and call 911.

How to prevent it

  • Have gas appliances and connectors inspected periodically by a licensed plumber, especially older water heaters and wall furnaces.
  • Know where your outside gas shutoff is and keep a properly sized wrench nearby for earthquakes.
  • Replace old, cracked, or kinked flexible appliance connectors rather than reusing them when you move an appliance.
  • Install UL-listed natural gas and carbon monoxide alarms, which give an early warning many people cannot smell.
  • After any earthquake, sniff for gas and check appliances before relighting pilots.

Frequently asked questions

What does natural gas smell like?

Natural gas is naturally odorless, so utilities add a chemical called mercaptan that smells like rotten eggs or sulfur. If you notice that smell, treat it as a possible gas leak.

Should I open windows before I leave?

Only if a window is right next to your exit path and opening it does not delay you. Your priority is to get out fast and leave doors open, not to ventilate the house. Never operate electric window mechanisms.

Can I turn off the gas myself?

You can turn the outside meter valve a quarter turn to off with a wrench if you can reach it safely on your way out, but never search for a leak or shut off gas in a crawlspace or basement. Let PG&E or the fire department handle the source.

The smell is faint and near the water heater. Is that still an emergency?

Yes. Even a faint gas odor should be taken seriously. Leave and call PG&E. A pilot may have gone out or a connector may be leaking, and only a professional should confirm the cause.
