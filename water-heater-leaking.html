Why Is My Water Heater Leaking?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Water Heaters · Sonoma County ]
Answer

A leaking water heater usually comes from one of five places: the temperature and pressure relief (T&P) valve, the drain valve at the bottom, the tank connections, condensation, or the steel tank itself. Water dripping from a valve or fitting is often a repairable fix, but water seeping from the base of the tank almost always means internal corrosion and the unit needs replacement. Shut off the water and, for gas units, the gas; then trace the source before deciding what to do next.

Most likely causes

  1. T&P (temperature/pressure) relief valve

    This safety valve opens and drips when tank pressure or temperature gets too high, or it can wear out and weep. A steady drip from the discharge tube often points to high thermostat settings, thermal expansion, or a failing valve.

  2. Drain valve at the base

    The plastic or brass drain spigot near the bottom can loosen or fail to seal after a flush, leaving a slow drip. This is a common and inexpensive fix.

  3. Tank corrosion and age

    After 8-12 years the steel tank rusts from the inside out, and Sonoma County's hard water speeds up sediment buildup and corrosion. Water pooling directly under the tank body signals the tank has failed and cannot be repaired.

  4. Loose or corroded connections

    The cold-inlet and hot-outlet nipples, unions, and flex lines on top of the heater can loosen or corrode and drip. These fittings can usually be tightened or replaced.

  5. Condensation

    On a newer unit, cold incoming water can cause harmless condensation on the tank, especially early in a heating cycle. It typically disappears once the water warms and is not a true leak.

How to find the source yourself

  1. Turn the thermostat to the lowest setting (gas) or switch off the breaker (electric) so the unit stops heating while you investigate.
  2. Dry the whole heater and the floor with towels, then place dry paper towels under likely spots.
  3. Wait 30-60 minutes without running hot water and see where wetness reappears.
  4. Check the T&P valve and its discharge tube: dampness here points to the valve or high pressure.
  5. Inspect the drain valve at the bottom and the flex connections on top for drips or corrosion.
  6. Look at the base of the tank itself: standing water under the tank body usually means the tank has rusted through.
  7. If water is actively pooling, shut off the cold-water supply valve above the heater to stop the flow.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if water is coming from the base of the tank, if the T&P valve drips repeatedly after you lower the temperature, or if you see rust-colored water or scorching near a gas burner. A leaking tank is not repairable and should be replaced promptly to avoid flooding and, on gas units, to keep venting safe. If you are ever unsure whether it is condensation or a real leak, a quick professional inspection is worth it.

How to prevent it

  • Flush the tank once a year (twice for very hard water) to clear sediment that accelerates corrosion.
  • Check and replace the anode rod every 3-5 years so it protects the tank instead of the steel.
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 F to reduce pressure stress and scald risk.
  • Consider a whole-home softener or scale treatment, since Sonoma County hard water shortens tank life.
  • Install a drip pan with a drain line if the heater sits in a closet, attic, or over living space.

Frequently asked questions

Is a leaking water heater dangerous?

A slow drip is mainly a water-damage and mold risk, but a leaking gas water heater can also create venting or ignition hazards, and a failing T&P valve signals dangerous pressure. Shut off the water and gas and address it quickly.

Can a leaking water heater be repaired?

Leaks from valves, fittings, or connections are usually repairable. A leak from the tank body itself means the steel has corroded through and the unit must be replaced.

Why is water dripping from the pipe on the side of my heater?

That is the T&P relief valve's discharge tube. It releases water when pressure or temperature is too high, so a persistent drip means high settings, thermal expansion, or a worn valve that needs attention.

How long do water heaters last in Sonoma County?

Tank heaters typically last 8-12 years, but the area's hard water can shorten that by accelerating sediment and corrosion. Annual flushing and anode checks help you reach the upper end of that range.

Should I turn off my water heater if it is leaking?

Yes. Shut off the cold-water supply valve above the unit, set gas to pilot or turn off the breaker, and stop the leak before it causes floor or wall damage.
