How to Prevent Frozen & Burst Pipes

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Emergency & Safety · Sonoma County ]
Answer

To prevent frozen and burst pipes, insulate the pipes most exposed to cold, disconnect and drain garden hoses before winter, and during an actual freeze let a faucet drip and keep your home heated. Sonoma County's inland and valley areas, Santa Rosa, Windsor, Healdsburg, get occasional hard freezes, and older homes here often have vulnerable pipes in crawlspaces and exterior walls. Knowing where your main water shutoff is, before a pipe bursts, is the single most valuable step, because it lets you stop the flooding fast.

Most likely causes

  1. Pipes in exterior walls

    Water lines running through outside-facing walls have little insulation between them and the cold. In older Sonoma homes these are a common freeze point during a hard valley freeze.

  2. Crawlspace and unheated-area pipes

    Pipes in vented crawlspaces, garages, attics, and under manufactured homes lose heat quickly because these spaces are not heated and cold air circulates freely.

  3. Outdoor hose bibs and sprinkler lines

    Exterior faucets (hose bibs) and irrigation lines are fully exposed. A hose left attached traps water in the bib, which freezes, expands, and can crack the valve or the pipe behind the wall.

How to prevent frozen and burst pipes

  1. Locate your main water shutoff now and make sure everyone in the household knows where it is and how to turn it off. It is usually where the water line enters the house or at the street meter (a meter key helps). This is your emergency stop if a pipe bursts.
  2. Insulate at-risk pipes with foam pipe sleeves or wrap, focusing on exterior walls, crawlspaces, the garage, and any unheated areas. Foam sleeves cost only a few dollars per length at the hardware store.
  3. Disconnect, drain, and store garden hoses before the first freeze, and shut off and drain any interior valve that feeds an outdoor faucet. Install insulated faucet covers on hose bibs.
  4. During a freeze warning, let a single faucet farthest from the shutoff drip slightly. Moving water is far less likely to freeze, and the trickle relieves pressure buildup that actually causes the pipe to burst.
  5. Keep your heat on and set no lower than 55 degrees F, even when away. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so household warmth reaches the pipes.
  6. Seal drafts near pipes: close crawlspace vents temporarily during a freeze and caulk gaps where cold air reaches plumbing.
  7. For extended trips in freezing weather, consider shutting off the water and draining the system, or have someone check the house.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a licensed plumber if a pipe has already frozen and you cannot safely thaw it, if a pipe has burst (shut off the main water first), if you have recurring freeze problems in the same spot, or if you want frost-proof hose bibs installed or pipes properly insulated in a hard-to-reach crawlspace. How to prevent it

  • Wrap vulnerable pipes with foam insulation before winter, especially in crawlspaces and exterior walls.
  • Remove and drain garden hoses every fall and cover hose bibs.
  • Learn the location of your main shutoff and keep a meter key handy.
  • During cold snaps, let a faucet drip and keep cabinet doors open under exterior-wall sinks.
  • Never set your thermostat below 55 degrees F in freezing weather, even when traveling.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if a pipe freezes but has not burst?

Keep the faucet open so water can flow as the ice melts, then apply gentle heat to the frozen section with a hair dryer, heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water, working from the faucet end toward the blockage. Never use an open flame or torch. If you cannot reach the frozen area or it will not thaw, call a plumber.

How do I know if a pipe is frozen?

Little or no water comes out when you open a faucet during cold weather, or you see frost on an exposed pipe. A bulge or crack in a pipe is a sign it has already been damaged by freezing.

Why does a frozen pipe burst?

Water expands as it freezes. The break usually is not caused by the ice pushing directly on the pipe wall, but by the pressure that builds up in the closed section between the ice blockage and a closed faucet. That is why letting a faucet drip helps: it relieves the pressure.

Does this really matter in Sonoma County?

Yes, in the inland and valley areas. Coastal Marin rarely freezes hard, but Santa Rosa, Windsor, Healdsburg, and other inland spots get occasional hard winter freezes, and older homes with exposed crawlspace pipes are genuinely at risk on those nights.
