How Much Does a Whole-Home Repipe Cost?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Costs & Decisions · Sonoma County ]
Answer

A whole-home repipe in Sonoma and Marin typically runs about $6,000 to $18,000, with most single-story homes landing near $8,000 to $12,000. PEX is usually the more affordable choice and copper the pricier, longer-lived option. Your final number depends mostly on the number of bathrooms, whether the home sits on a slab or a crawlspace, and how much drywall has to be opened and repaired afterward.

Most likely causes

  1. Number of bathrooms and fixtures

    Each bathroom, kitchen, laundry hookup, and hose bib adds pipe runs and labor. A two-bath home costs less to repipe than a four-bath home simply because there are fewer lines to route.

  2. Slab vs. crawlspace or attic access

    Homes with an accessible crawlspace or attic are cheaper to repipe because plumbers can route new lines without cutting into concrete. Slab foundations often require rerouting lines overhead through walls and ceilings, which raises labor and drywall costs.

  3. Pipe material chosen

    PEX is flexible, quick to install, and resists the scale buildup common with Sonoma County hard water. Copper costs more in both material and labor but is time-tested and preferred by some homeowners. The material choice can shift the total by several thousand dollars.

  4. Drywall repair and finishing

    Opening walls to reach pipes is part of the job, but patching, texturing, and painting afterward is often a separate line item. On finished, occupied homes this can be a meaningful share of the total.

  5. Permits and inspection

    A permitted repipe includes permit fees and an inspection by the local building department. This protects you at resale and ensures the work meets code, but it is a real cost to budget for.

What drives your repipe quote

  1. Count your bathrooms and major fixtures so the estimate reflects the true number of pipe runs.
  2. Note your foundation type: crawlspace and attic access lower cost, slab foundations tend to raise it.
  3. Decide between PEX and copper based on budget, longevity goals, and preference.
  4. Ask whether drywall patching and paint are included or quoted separately.
  5. Confirm the quote includes a permit and final inspection.

When to call a licensed plumber

Repiping is a whole-house job that involves permits, opening walls, and pressure-testing new lines. It is worth a licensed plumber's site visit for an accurate quote. Call for a professional assessment if your home still has galvanized steel or polybutylene pipe, or if you are getting repeated leaks.

How to prevent it

  • If you have older galvanized pipe, plan ahead rather than waiting for a burst; galvanized corrodes from the inside and eventually restricts flow and rusts water.
  • Consider a repipe when you are already remodeling a bathroom or kitchen, since walls are open and labor overlaps.
  • Install a water softener or conditioner after a copper repipe to slow scale buildup from Sonoma County's hard water.
  • Keep your household water pressure in a safe range with a pressure-reducing valve to extend the life of new pipe.

Frequently asked questions

Why do so many older Sonoma homes need a repipe?

Homes built before the 1970s often have galvanized steel pipe, which corrodes internally over decades and produces rusty water, low pressure, and eventually leaks. Some homes from the late 1970s through mid-1990s have polybutylene, which is prone to failure. Both are common reasons to repipe here.

Is PEX or copper better for hard water?

PEX resists mineral scale and does not corrode, which is an advantage with Sonoma County's hard water. Copper is durable and long-lived but can be more affected by scale and aggressive water chemistry over time. Both are code-approved; the right choice depends on your budget and preferences.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most single-family repipes take two to five working days, plus separate time for drywall patching and paint. Homes on slab foundations or with more bathrooms sit at the longer end.

Will I have water during the repipe?

Water is usually shut off during working hours and restored each evening in most cases, though this depends on the layout. Your plumber will walk you through the daily schedule before starting.
