Why Is My Drain Clogged (and How to Clear It)?

CL Reviewed by Cody Lehman · Updated August 2026

[ Drains & Sewer · Sonoma County ]
Answer

A clogged drain is almost always caused by something physically narrowing or blocking the pipe: hair and soap scum in tubs and showers, grease and food in kitchen sinks, or non-flushable items in toilets. If just one fixture drains slowly, the clog is local and you can usually clear it yourself with a plunger, hot water, or a hand snake. If every drain in the house is slow or backing up at the same time, the problem is likely in your main sewer line, which needs a pro.

Most likely causes

  1. Hair and soap scum (tubs and showers)

    Hair binds with soap and body oils to form a mat that catches on the crossbars of the drain and inside the P-trap. This is the single most common bathroom clog and builds up slowly over months.

  2. Grease, oil, and food (kitchen sinks)

    Fats poured down the drain cool and harden on pipe walls, then trap coffee grounds, starchy food, and fibrous scraps. Garbage disposals do not stop this; ground food still accumulates downstream.

  3. Flushed items (toilets)

    Wet wipes labeled 'flushable,' paper towels, cotton products, dental floss, and toys do not break down like toilet paper and lodge in the trap or the branch line.

  4. Hard-water mineral scale

    Sonoma County's hard water leaves calcium and magnesium deposits that gradually narrow pipe diameter, so drains that were fine for years slowly get sluggish.

  5. Blocked or undersized vent

    Every drain needs air from a roof vent to flow properly. A vent blocked by leaves, a bird nest, or a wasp nest causes glugging, slow draining, and gurgling even when the pipe itself is clear.

  6. Main sewer line issue

    If several fixtures are slow at once, or the lowest drain backs up when you run water elsewhere, the blockage is in the shared main line, often from tree roots in older clay laterals common in Santa Rosa and Petaluma.

How to check it yourself

  1. Identify the pattern first: is it one fixture or all of them? One slow drain is local; multiple slow drains or a backup at the lowest drain points to the main line, so stop and call a pro.
  2. For a single clog, remove and clean the visible stopper or strainer; pull out hair with a bent-wire hook or a plastic drain-cleaning strip.
  3. Try a plunger: for a sink, block the overflow hole or the second basin with a wet rag, add enough water to cover the plunger cup, and give firm sustained plunges.
  4. Flush a slow (not fully blocked) drain with a kettle of hot but not boiling water; boiling water can soften PVC and loosen joints.
  5. Try baking soda and vinegar: pour about half a cup of baking soda, then half a cup of white vinegar, wait 15 minutes, and flush with hot water. This is mild and safe for pipes.
  6. For a stubborn local clog, run a hand-crank drain snake (auger) into the pipe, turning as you feed it to catch and pull out the debris.
  7. Do NOT use chemical drain cleaners. They generate heat that can warp plastic pipes and corrode older metal, they rarely clear a full blockage, and leftover caustic makes any later pro service hazardous.

When to call a licensed plumber

Call a professional if more than one fixture is slow, if the lowest drain (often a downstairs shower or floor drain) backs up when you flush a toilet or run the washer, if you hear gurgling from other drains, or if sewage or foul water appears. These are signs of a main-line blockage, not a local clog, and continuing to run water can flood the lowest drain. Also call if a single clog does not clear after plunging and snaking, since it may be a deeper break or a belly in the pipe.

How to prevent it

  • Use a mesh or basket strainer in every tub, shower, and sink to catch hair and food.
  • Never pour grease, oil, or bacon fat down the drain; wipe pans with a paper towel and trash it, or collect grease in a can.
  • Flush only the '3 Ps': pee, poop, and (toilet) paper. Skip wipes even if labeled flushable.
  • Run hot water down the kitchen sink for 15 seconds after washing greasy dishes.
  • Once a month, do a baking-soda-and-hot-water flush on each drain to keep buildup down.
  • In older homes with clay laterals, schedule periodic camera inspections to catch root intrusion before it becomes a full backup.

Frequently asked questions

Are chemical drain cleaners safe to use once in a while?

It is best to avoid them entirely. The lye and acid formulas create heat that can distort PVC and eat away at older galvanized or cast-iron pipe, and they often just sit on top of a clog. If a plumber later has to open the pipe, residual chemicals become a burn and splash hazard.

Why is only my kitchen sink slow but everything else is fine?

That points to a local clog in the kitchen branch line, usually grease and food buildup in the P-trap or the horizontal run. Try plunging, hot water, and a hand snake before calling anyone.

All my drains are slow at once. What does that mean?

Simultaneous slow drains, gurgling, or a backup at your lowest fixture almost always means the main sewer line is partially blocked, frequently by tree roots in older Sonoma County clay laterals. Stop running water and have the line camera-inspected.

Does a garbage disposal prevent clogs?

No. It grinds food finer, but the particles still travel down the same pipe and can accumulate, especially with grease, coffee grounds, rice, pasta, and fibrous scraps like celery.
